# Angular Momentum and the Hydrogen Atom

> **Prerequisites**: 01_postulates_of_quantum_mechanics.md (observables, commutators), 02_qubits_and_the_bloch_sphere.md (Pauli matrices, SU(2) rotations), 06_wave_mechanics_and_schrodinger.md (TISE, boundary conditions), 04_entanglement_and_nonlocality.md (Bell states)
> **Connects to**: The qubit itself — spin-½ *is* the original qubit; docs/07_quantum_hardware/02_trapped_ion_qubits.md (hyperfine and Zeeman qubits are angular-momentum states of atoms), docs/03_quantum_gates_and_circuits (SU(2) rotations)

## Overview

Angular momentum in quantum mechanics is a triumph of pure algebra: from the single commutation relation `[Ĵᵢ, Ĵⱼ] = iℏ ε_{ijk} Ĵₖ` — and nothing else — the entire spectrum follows, including the surprise that half-integer values are allowed. Those half-integer representations are spin, and the smallest one, spin-½, is mathematically *identical* to the qubit of Chapter 2.2. The Bloch sphere is not merely analogous to a spinning particle; a spin-½ in a magnetic field is the original physical qubit, and the `SU(2)` rotation formalism you learned for gates is exactly the physics of magnetic resonance.

This chapter derives the `|j, m⟩` tower from the algebra, distinguishes orbital from spin angular momentum, works out how two angular momenta combine (Clebsch-Gordan coefficients), and shows that combining two spin-½ particles produces the singlet and triplet — with the singlet being none other than the Bell state `|Ψ⁻⟩` of Chapter 2.4. We then assemble the hydrogen atom, whose energy levels `E_n = −13.6 eV/n²` and quantum numbers `(n, l, m)` define the natural qubit registers of atomic platforms.

## The Angular Momentum Algebra

### From commutators to the spectrum

Define an angular momentum as any triple of Hermitian operators satisfying

`[Ĵ_x, Ĵ_y] = iℏĴ_z`,  `[Ĵ_y, Ĵ_z] = iℏĴ_x`,  `[Ĵ_z, Ĵ_x] = iℏĴ_y`

(compactly `[Ĵᵢ, Ĵⱼ] = iℏ ε_{ijk} Ĵₖ`). Orbital angular momentum `L̂ = r̂ × p̂` satisfies this as a consequence of `[x̂,p̂] = iℏ`; spin satisfies it by definition. The Pauli relations `[X, Y] = 2iZ` from Chapter 2.2 are this algebra with `Ĵ = (ℏ/2)σ`.

The total angular momentum squared, `Ĵ² = Ĵ_x² + Ĵ_y² + Ĵ_z²`, commutes with every component: `[Ĵ², Ĵᵢ] = 0`. So we can simultaneously diagonalize `Ĵ²` and one component, conventionally `Ĵ_z`. Label the joint eigenstates `|j, m⟩`:

`Ĵ²|j, m⟩ = ℏ²j(j+1)|j, m⟩`,  `Ĵ_z|j, m⟩ = ℏm|j, m⟩`

### Ladder operators and the |j, m⟩ tower

Define `Ĵ± = Ĵ_x ± iĴ_y`. From the algebra:

`[Ĵ_z, Ĵ±] = ±ℏĴ±`,  `[Ĵ+, Ĵ−] = 2ℏĴ_z`,  `[Ĵ², Ĵ±] = 0`

So `Ĵ±` raise/lower `m` by one while leaving `j` fixed — the same ladder logic as the oscillator's `â†, â` (Chapter 2.7), but now the ladder is *bounded at both ends*. The norm computation

`‖Ĵ±|j,m⟩‖² = ⟨j,m|Ĵ∓Ĵ±|j,m⟩ = ℏ²[j(j+1) − m(m±1)]`

must be non-negative, forcing `−j ≤ m ≤ j`. Climbing from the bottom rung `m = −j` to the top rung `m = +j` in unit steps requires `2j` to be a non-negative integer. Therefore:

`j ∈ {0, ½, 1, 3/2, 2, …}`,  `m ∈ {−j, −j+1, …, +j}` (that is `2j+1` values)

with matrix elements

`Ĵ±|j, m⟩ = ℏ√(j(j+1) − m(m±1)) |j, m±1⟩`

This is the complete solution: every angular momentum in nature is a `(2j+1)`-dimensional block of this universal structure.

### Orbital vs spin

- **Orbital** angular momentum `L̂ = r̂ × p̂` acts on wavefunctions. Requiring single-valuedness of `ψ(θ, φ)` under `φ → φ + 2π` restricts orbital `l` to **integers**; the eigenfunctions are the spherical harmonics `Y_l^m(θ, φ)`.
- **Spin** `Ŝ` has no position-space realization — it acts on an internal `(2s+1)`-dimensional space and may take **half-integer** values. Under a `2π` rotation a half-integer spin state acquires a phase `−1` (the `SU(2) → SO(3)` double cover from Chapter 2.2), which is unobservable globally but detectable interferometrically.

### Spin-½ is the qubit

For `j = ½` the two states `|½, +½⟩ ≡ |↑⟩` and `|½, −½⟩ ≡ |↓⟩` span `ℂ²`, and the general matrix elements above reduce to

`Ŝ = (ℏ/2)σ`,  i.e.  `Ŝ_x = (ℏ/2)X`, `Ŝ_y = (ℏ/2)Y`, `Ŝ_z = (ℏ/2)Z`

Identify `|↑⟩ = |0⟩`, `|↓⟩ = |1⟩` and the dictionary is complete: the Bloch vector of Chapter 2.2 is literally the spin direction `⟨Ŝ⟩/(ℏ/2)`; the rotation gates `R_n̂(θ) = e^{−iθ n̂·σ/2}` are `e^{−iθ n̂·Ĵ/ℏ}`, physical rotations generated by angular momentum; and precession of a spin in a magnetic field `B ẑ` (Hamiltonian `Ĥ = −γB Ŝ_z`) is a continuously applied `R_z` gate. Spin-½ in a field is the archetype of every two-level qubit.

## Addition of Angular Momenta

### The general problem

Two subsystems with angular momenta `j₁` and `j₂` have total `Ĵ = Ĵ₁ + Ĵ₂` (meaning `Ĵ₁ ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ Ĵ₂`). Two natural bases exist for the `(2j₁+1)(2j₂+1)`-dimensional space:

- **Product basis** `|j₁, m₁⟩|j₂, m₂⟩` — eigenstates of `Ĵ₁z` and `Ĵ₂z`
- **Coupled basis** `|J, M⟩` — eigenstates of `Ĵ²` and `Ĵ_z`, where `J` runs over

`J = |j₁ − j₂|, |j₁ − j₂| + 1, …, j₁ + j₂`

(check: `Σ (2J+1) = (2j₁+1)(2j₂+1)` ✓). The unitary change of basis is given by the **Clebsch-Gordan coefficients** `⟨j₁ m₁; j₂ m₂ | J M⟩`, nonzero only when `M = m₁ + m₂`.

### ½ ⊗ ½ = 1 ⊕ 0

For two spin-½ particles the four-dimensional space splits into a **triplet** (`J = 1`, three states) and a **singlet** (`J = 0`, one state). This decomposition is derived in full in the worked example below; the result:

- Triplet: `|1,1⟩ = |↑↑⟩`, `|1,0⟩ = (|↑↓⟩ + |↓↑⟩)/√2`, `|1,−1⟩ = |↓↓⟩` — symmetric under particle exchange
- Singlet: `|0,0⟩ = (|↑↓⟩ − |↓↑⟩)/√2` — antisymmetric

**Connection to Bell states** (Chapter 2.4): with `|↑⟩ = |0⟩`, `|↓⟩ = |1⟩`,

`|0,0⟩ = (|01⟩ − |10⟩)/√2 = |Ψ⁻⟩`  and  `|1,0⟩ = (|01⟩ + |10⟩)/√2 = |Ψ⁺⟩`

The singlet is the Bell state `|Ψ⁻⟩`. Its defining property `Ĵ|0,0⟩ = 0` (zero *total* angular momentum) means it is **rotationally invariant**: `(U ⊗ U)|Ψ⁻⟩ = det(U)|Ψ⁻⟩ ~ |Ψ⁻⟩` for any `U ∈ SU(2)`. This is why singlet measurements are perfectly anticorrelated along *every* axis — the property exploited in the CHSH/Bell-inequality analysis of Chapter 2.4, and the reason decoherence-free subspaces encode qubits in singlet-like states. (`|Φ±⟩ = (|00⟩ ± |11⟩)/√2` are `M = ±1` combinations within the triplet sector: `|Φ±⟩ = (|1,1⟩ ± |1,−1⟩)/√2`.)

## The Hydrogen Atom

### Separation and the radial equation

An electron bound to a proton by `V(r) = −e²/(4πε₀ r)` has a rotationally invariant Hamiltonian, so `Ĥ`, `L̂²`, `L̂_z` commute and eigenfunctions factor as

`ψ_{nlm}(r, θ, φ) = R_{nl}(r) Y_l^m(θ, φ)`

Writing `u(r) = rR(r)`, the radial part becomes a 1D Schrödinger problem on `r > 0`:

`−(ℏ²/2m) u'' + [V(r) + ℏ²l(l+1)/(2mr²)] u = E u`

— an effective potential combining Coulomb attraction with a centrifugal barrier. Solving by the same series-termination logic as the oscillator (Chapter 2.7): the series for `u` must terminate for normalizability, which quantizes the energy.

### Spectrum and quantum numbers

`E_n = −(m e⁴)/(2(4πε₀)²ℏ²) · (1/n²) = −13.6 eV / n²`,  `n = 1, 2, 3, …`

with quantum numbers:

- `n` (principal): `n = 1, 2, …` — sets the energy and the size scale `⟨r⟩ ~ n²a₀`, with Bohr radius `a₀ = 4πε₀ℏ²/(me²) = 0.529 Å`
- `l` (orbital): `l = 0, 1, …, n−1` — the s, p, d, f shells
- `m` (magnetic): `m = −l, …, +l`

The energy depends only on `n` — an "accidental" degeneracy special to the `1/r` potential. The degeneracy of level `n` is

`Σ_{l=0}^{n−1} (2l+1) = n²`  (or `2n²` counting spin)

Check: `n = 1` holds 2 electrons (helium's shell), `n = 2` holds 8 — the periodic table's structure falls out.

### Fine structure (one paragraph)

Relativistic corrections — the kinetic-energy correction `p⁴` term, spin-orbit coupling `~ L̂·Ŝ`, and the Darwin term — split the `n²`-fold degeneracy at relative order `α² ≈ (1/137)² ≈ 5×10⁻⁵`, grouping states by total `j = l ± ½` with `E_{nj} ≈ E_n[1 + (α²/n²)(n/(j+½) − ¾)]`. Beyond that lie the Lamb shift (QED) and **hyperfine structure**, the coupling of electron and nuclear spins — famously the 21 cm line of hydrogen, and practically the level splitting that defines the clock-state qubits of trapped-ion and neutral-atom processors (Chapter 7.2). When you read that an ion qubit is encoded in "hyperfine states `|F, m_F⟩`", `F` is exactly the coupled angular momentum `J + I` of this chapter's addition rules.

## Key Formulas

- Algebra: `[Ĵᵢ, Ĵⱼ] = iℏ ε_{ijk} Ĵₖ`; `[Ĵ², Ĵᵢ] = 0`
- Spectrum: `Ĵ²|j,m⟩ = ℏ²j(j+1)|j,m⟩`, `Ĵ_z|j,m⟩ = ℏm|j,m⟩`; `j` integer or half-integer, `m = −j…j`
- Ladders: `Ĵ±|j,m⟩ = ℏ√(j(j+1) − m(m±1))|j,m±1⟩`
- Spin-½: `Ŝ = (ℏ/2)σ`; rotations `e^{−iθn̂·Ŝ/ℏ} = R_n̂(θ)`
- Addition: `j₁ ⊗ j₂ → J = |j₁−j₂|, …, j₁+j₂`; CG coefficients `⟨j₁m₁; j₂m₂|JM⟩`, `M = m₁+m₂`
- Two qubits: `½ ⊗ ½ = 1 ⊕ 0`; singlet `(|01⟩−|10⟩)/√2 = |Ψ⁻⟩`
- Hydrogen: `E_n = −13.6 eV/n²`; `l < n`, `|m| ≤ l`; degeneracy `n²` (or `2n²` with spin); `a₀ = 0.529 Å`

## Worked Example: Deriving ½ ⊗ ½ = 1 ⊕ 0 with Explicit CG Coefficients

**Problem**: Construct all four `|J, M⟩` states of two spin-½ particles from the product basis `{|↑↑⟩, |↑↓⟩, |↓↑⟩, |↓↓⟩}` using ladder operators, and identify them with Bell-basis states.

**Solution**:

**Step 1 — top of the ladder.** The unique state with `M = m₁ + m₂ = 1` is `|↑↑⟩`. Since `M = 1` requires `J ≥ 1` and the maximum possible `J` is `1`:

`|1, 1⟩ = |↑↑⟩`  (CG coefficient `⟨½½;½½|1,1⟩ = 1`)

**Step 2 — lower with `Ĵ− = Ĵ₁− + Ĵ₂−`.** Using `Ĵ−|1,1⟩ = ℏ√(1·2 − 1·0)|1,0⟩ = ℏ√2 |1,0⟩` on the left, and on the right (each `Ĵᵢ−` flips one spin with coefficient `ℏ√(½·3/2 − ½·(−½)) = ℏ`):

`Ĵ−|↑↑⟩ = ℏ(|↓↑⟩ + |↑↓⟩)`

Equating: `|1, 0⟩ = (|↑↓⟩ + |↓↑⟩)/√2` — CG coefficients `⟨½,±½; ½,∓½|1,0⟩ = 1/√2`.

**Step 3 — lower again.** `Ĵ−|1,0⟩ = ℏ√2|1,−1⟩`, while applying `Ĵ−` to `(|↑↓⟩+|↓↑⟩)/√2` gives `(ℏ|↓↓⟩ + ℏ|↓↓⟩)/√2 = ℏ√2|↓↓⟩`. So

`|1, −1⟩ = |↓↓⟩`

**Step 4 — the singlet by orthogonality.** The remaining state must have `M = 0` and be orthogonal to `|1,0⟩` in the two-dimensional `{|↑↓⟩, |↓↑⟩}` subspace. Up to phase convention (Condon–Shortley: the `m₁ = +½` coefficient positive):

`|0, 0⟩ = (|↑↓⟩ − |↓↑⟩)/√2` — CG coefficients `⟨½,½;½,−½|0,0⟩ = +1/√2`, `⟨½,−½;½,½|0,0⟩ = −1/√2`

Verify it is annihilated by both ladders: `Ĵ−|0,0⟩ = ℏ(|↓↓⟩ − |↓↓⟩)/√2 = 0`, and similarly `Ĵ+|0,0⟩ = 0`, confirming `J = 0`.

**Step 5 — numerical verification.** Building `Ŝ² = (Ŝ₁+Ŝ₂)²` and `Ŝ_z` as `4×4` matrices (units `ℏ = 1`) and evaluating expectation values:

| state | `⟨Ŝ²⟩` (expect `J(J+1)`) | `⟨Ŝ_z⟩` (expect `M`) |
|---|---|---|
| `\|↑↑⟩` | 2.0000 | +1.0000 |
| `(\|↑↓⟩+\|↓↑⟩)/√2` | 2.0000 | 0.0000 |
| `\|↓↓⟩` | 2.0000 | −1.0000 |
| `(\|↑↓⟩−\|↓↑⟩)/√2` | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |

All four check out.

**Step 6 — Bell identification.** With `|↑⟩ = |0⟩`, `|↓⟩ = |1⟩`:

- Singlet `|0,0⟩ = |Ψ⁻⟩` — the maximally entangled, rotationally invariant Bell state
- Triplet `|1,0⟩ = |Ψ⁺⟩`
- Triplet combinations `(|1,1⟩ ± |1,−1⟩)/√2 = (|00⟩ ± |11⟩)/√2 = |Φ±⟩`

The Bell basis is a (partial) recoupling of the angular-momentum basis: entanglement classification and angular momentum addition are two views of the same change of basis.

## Summary

- The commutator algebra `[Ĵᵢ,Ĵⱼ] = iℏε_{ijk}Ĵₖ` alone fixes the spectrum: `Ĵ² → ℏ²j(j+1)`, `Ĵ_z → ℏm`, with `j` integer *or half-integer* and `m = −j, …, +j`
- `Ĵ± = Ĵ_x ± iĴ_y` climb the `2j+1`-rung tower with matrix elements `ℏ√(j(j+1) − m(m±1))`
- Orbital angular momentum (integer `l`, spherical harmonics) acts on wavefunctions; spin is internal and can be half-integer
- Spin-½ **is** the qubit: `Ŝ = (ℏ/2)σ`, the Bloch vector is the spin direction, gates are physical rotations
- Adding angular momenta: `J` runs from `|j₁−j₂|` to `j₁+j₂`; for two qubits `½⊗½ = 1⊕0`, and the `J = 0` singlet is the Bell state `|Ψ⁻⟩` — rotationally invariant and perfectly anticorrelated along every axis
- Hydrogen: `E_n = −13.6 eV/n²` with quantum numbers `n > l ≥ |m|` and degeneracy `2n²`; fine and hyperfine structure split these levels and furnish the qubit states of atomic hardware

## Exercises

**Exercise 1**: Show `[Ĵ², Ĵ_z] = 0` directly from the algebra.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

`[Ĵ_x², Ĵ_z] = Ĵ_x[Ĵ_x, Ĵ_z] + [Ĵ_x, Ĵ_z]Ĵ_x = −iℏ(Ĵ_xĴ_y + Ĵ_yĴ_x)`.
`[Ĵ_y², Ĵ_z] = Ĵ_y[Ĵ_y, Ĵ_z] + [Ĵ_y, Ĵ_z]Ĵ_y = +iℏ(Ĵ_yĴ_x + Ĵ_xĴ_y)`.
`[Ĵ_z², Ĵ_z] = 0`. The first two cancel, so `[Ĵ², Ĵ_z] = 0`. By symmetry the same holds for `Ĵ_x` and `Ĵ_y` — but note `Ĵ_x, Ĵ_y, Ĵ_z` do not commute with *each other*, so only one component can be sharp at a time.
</details>

**Exercise 2**: For spin-½, verify `Ŝ±` computed from `Ŝ = (ℏ/2)σ` match the general ladder formula.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

`Ŝ+ = Ŝ_x + iŜ_y = (ℏ/2)(X + iY) = ℏ [[0,1],[0,0]]` (in the `{|↑⟩,|↓⟩}` basis). So `Ŝ+|↓⟩ = ℏ|↑⟩`, `Ŝ+|↑⟩ = 0`.

General formula with `j = ½`, `m = −½`: `ℏ√(½·(3/2) − (−½)(+½)) = ℏ√(3/4 + 1/4) = ℏ` ✓. And for `m = +½` the ladder terminates: `√(3/4 − (½)(3/2)) = 0` ✓.
</details>

**Exercise 3**: A system of two qubits is in the singlet. Alice measures her spin along an arbitrary axis `n̂` and gets `+ℏ/2`. What does Bob get if he measures along the same axis? Prove it using rotational invariance.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Rotational invariance: `(U ⊗ U)|Ψ⁻⟩ = det(U)|Ψ⁻⟩`, a global phase for `U ∈ SU(2)`. Choose `U` rotating `ẑ → n̂`; then the singlet has the same form `(|↑↓⟩_n − |↓↑⟩_n)/√2` in the `n̂`-basis. If Alice finds `+` along `n̂`, the state collapses to `|↑⟩_n|↓⟩_n`, and Bob finds `−ℏ/2` with certainty: perfect anticorrelation along *every* axis. The triplet `|1,0⟩ = |Ψ⁺⟩` anticorrelates in the `z`-basis too, but *correlates* in the `x`-basis — only the singlet anticorrelates universally. This universal anticorrelation is the input to the EPR argument and the CHSH analysis of Chapter 2.4.
</details>

**Exercise 4**: What total angular momenta arise from combining `j₁ = 1` and `j₂ = ½`? Verify dimensions, and construct `|3/2, 3/2⟩` and `|3/2, 1/2⟩`.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

`J = 3/2` or `1/2`; dimensions `4 + 2 = 6 = 3 × 2` ✓.

Top state: `|3/2, 3/2⟩ = |1,1⟩|½,½⟩`. Lower with `Ĵ− = Ĵ₁− + Ĵ₂−`: left side gives `ℏ√(15/4 − 3/4)|3/2,½⟩ = ℏ√3|3/2,½⟩`; right side gives `ℏ√2 |1,0⟩|½,½⟩ + ℏ|1,1⟩|½,−½⟩`. So

`|3/2, ½⟩ = √(2/3) |1,0⟩|½,½⟩ + √(1/3) |1,1⟩|½,−½⟩`

(The orthogonal combination, in the Condon–Shortley convention, is `|½,½⟩ = √(2/3)|1,1⟩|½,−½⟩ − √(1/3)|1,0⟩|½,½⟩`.) These are the CG coefficients governing, e.g., p-shell fine structure `l = 1` with spin-½.
</details>

**Exercise 5**: How many states does hydrogen have with energy `E = −13.6/9 eV`? List them, then give the wavelength of the photon emitted in the `n = 3 → n = 2` transition.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

`E = −13.6/9 eV` means `n = 3`. Allowed `l = 0, 1, 2` with `2l+1` values of `m` each: `1 + 3 + 5 = 9` spatial states, `18` with spin — the `n² (2n²)` degeneracy.

Transition energy: `ΔE = 13.6(1/4 − 1/9) = 13.6 · 5/36 = 1.889 eV`. Wavelength `λ = hc/ΔE = 1240 eV·nm / 1.889 eV = 656 nm` — the red Balmer-α line, the most prominent line in the visible hydrogen spectrum.
</details>
